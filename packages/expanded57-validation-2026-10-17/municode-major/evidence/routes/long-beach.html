Zoning Regulations & Design Guidelines

The Zoning Regulations guide how a city develops. It is a set of rules that implements the General Plan on each parcel. It defines what any given parcel can be used for (e.g., housing, manufacturing, open space) and how buildings can be developed and designed. The first step is knowing in which zoning district you are located. When researching a site for a construction project or new business, be sure to ask the following:

  • How is the property zoned?
  • How is the building proposed to be used?
  • What are the uses allowed by the Zoning Regulations?
  • Does the proposed project require an entitlement approval?
  • What are the parking and landscape requirements?
  • Is the property in a historic district, or is it a designated historic landmark?



What Is My Zoning Designation? What Does That Mean?

Step One: Check the Zoning Map

  • Find out what your zoning designation is by accessing the Zoning and Land Use GIS Map that has layers to show Zoning District, General Plan PlaceType, Historic District and Coastal Zone.
  • View all relevant Zoning & Land Use Maps for more Planning information.

Step Two: Review Zoning Regulations

  • Cross reference the Zoning Regulations for a particular Zoning District to see the permitted activities, facilities, and development standards.

Parking Requirements

To check if your site is exempt from parking requirements, please visit the Parking Exempt Area page. If your site isn’t in the parking exempt area, please visit Chapter 21.41 of the Zoning Regulations to view requirements for off-street parking and loading.

Specific Plan Districts

Some areas of the City are "zoned" as special districts, called Specific Plan Districts. A Specific Plan is a comprehensive planning and zoning document that provides a vision, zoning regulations and design guidelines for the development and improvement of a specific neighborhood or corridor within Long Beach.
